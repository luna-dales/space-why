Where Space Why began

From launches to questions

I enjoyed building and flying rockets at Space Camp and with my family. Following SpaceX launches and watching model-rocketry videos made me curious about how rockets steer and land.

Joe Barnard's BPS. Space project inspired me to try something similar. Self-landing is my long-term inspiration. My work so far is learning the electronics, software, fabrication, and testing behind those systems.

An accessibility exploration

Making flight data easier to read—and hear

My experience with low vision has prompted me to explore alternative colors, adjustable font and image sizes, and audio feedback for flight simulations.

I used AI to explore interface options and create a viewer prototype. This remains an early design exploration, with more development and user testing ahead.

Accessibility also shapes my separate iPhone project, WhereIsIt.

Contributions & support

What I did, and how I learned

For Space Why, I assembled electronics, laid out schematics and PCBs, wrote flight-computer software, built rockets, and ran test flights. I also printed and assembled parts for the TVC platform and revised mounts after they broke.

My dad taught me soldering, helped me learn Autodesk Fusion, and got me started on Arduino C++ Sketch programming. I learned about coding and component choices through YouTube and internet resources. BPS.Space inspired the project and designed the TVC gimbal; I downloaded its STL files, 3D printed the parts, and assembled the gimbal. My test-platform work and mount revisions are documented separately.

V2 software now records separate sensor sessions, preserves raw readings, and supports repeatable analysis and gyro-offset checks. An experimental barometer-only Kalman estimator has passed software checks, with measured-height comparisons next. Explore the V2 software progression and results, with code in the project repository.

Next experiments

Validate the measurements and fine tune control.

Next steps for Flying Blind are calibration, reliable recording, and altitude-estimation tests against measured heights. The proven V2 software will then move to V3’s PCB. Future Green Egg flights will fine tune calibration and logging; TVC control and an instrumented firing remain development goals. I completed the Zephyr certification flight with Tripoli and earned my M1 certificate.
