Beyond the workbench / Making by hand

Crafting, one project at a time.

Baking, crocheting, and metal jewelry making are other ways I explore how things are made. They give me room to practice, try new ideas, and share what I learn.

Baking: learning to teachingCrocheting: stitches to charactersMetal jewelry: shaping and detail

01 / Baking

From learning in the kitchen to helping someone else learn.

My baking story started with learning alongside an adult. Over time, I became more independent, tried more detailed decorating, and began helping a younger child take part. These photos bring together those stages.

Learning the basics

Early projects gave me a place to practice: mixing, working at the kitchen counter, and decorating a cake. The finished cake was only part of the experience; learning the steps mattered too.

Taking the interest further

An adult baking class at Milk Bar

I also took an adult baking class at Milk Bar, extending my learning beyond the home kitchen. Layered cakes and detailed decorating became part of a growing collection of baking projects.

See Milk Bar’s Birthday Cake

Working independently and adding detail

Baking became a way to combine making something to eat with making something expressive. Cakes, frog cupcakes, and decorated cookies let me work with color, shape, and small details.

Sharing the skill

Becoming the person who helps

Eventually, I was the one helping a younger child in the kitchen. Teaching brings another kind of practice: slowing down, explaining a step, and making room for someone else to try.

02 / Crocheting

Turning stitches into characters.

Crocheting gives me a different way to build. A project grows through repeated stitches, then takes on a personality through its shape, colors, and details. The collection includes practice in progress as well as finished animals and other playful objects.

A growing collection

Working across different projects lets me explore long shapes, rounded bodies, small features, and contrasting colors. Crocheting is also something I can carry with me and return to a little at a time.

More crochet projects and practice

03 / Metal jewelry making

Shaping something I can wear.

Metal jewelry adds another material to my crafting. Curves, wrapped wire, and small decorative elements come together in a piece that has to work as a wearable shape as well as a design.

Small details, shared projects

The arm piece combines an open wire outline with butterflies and hanging flowers. The workshop and bracelet photos show the social side of making, too: working around a table and sharing the finished pieces.

What connects these interests

Practice, care, and sharing.

Across baking, crochet, jewelry, and engineering, I keep returning to making things with my hands. Each project offers a chance to get more comfortable with a material, pay closer attention to a detail, and share the result with someone else.
